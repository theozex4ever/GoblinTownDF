P2 is judged on tests 1 and 2. Test 1 is yes; test 2 is no. Tests 3, 4 and 5 match the P1 baseline.

The missed test 2 has a known cause: P2 goblins have no goals that compete. Each one takes the nearest Job and hauls it to the nearest Pile with room, so the warren does what the player expects. The only friction is a full Pile, and the player sees that coming. Nothing yet pulls a goblin away from its work, so nothing unplanned can happen. Needs (P3) and Chaos (P4) are the systems meant to supply that pull.

**Observed:** when every Pile is full of a goblin's material mid-haul, the goblin stands still holding its unit until the player paints more Piles. This is the designed behaviour (GDD → P2 decisions → Waiting hauls), but in play it reads as a frozen goblin rather than a goblin with an opinion. Decision: P2 stays as built. P3 shaping takes this as an input: a goblin stuck waiting should get bored (it should count against a Need, or later raise its Chaos), so the wait becomes something that happens to the goblin.

**The moment worth telling a friend about:** Goblins hauling dirt and stone to the Piles.

**Verdict:** Go, with a watch on test 2. Three clear yeses. Tests 2 and 3 are both no, which is the Tune pattern, but there are no chaos, quirk or need systems yet to tune. P2 is the second milestone in a row where test 2 is no. If it is still no after P3, apply Tune before P4.

**Next action:** Shape P3 into a P3 decisions block in the GDD. Include at least one rule where a Need pulls a goblin off its Job (for example, a hungry goblin abandons a dig to go and eat), so that the warren can surprise the player. Settle what a waiting carrier's boredom does. Judge P3 on test 2.
